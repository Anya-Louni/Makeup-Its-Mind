Values without
bindings

Steering a language model moves the value of an attribute. It leaves the region that attribute belongs to alone.

Domain makeup routine, 4 face regions, 3 attributes Models gpt2-124M, pythia-410m, Qwen3-0.6B-Base Narratives 7,400 Positions 83,655
0

Abstract

A concept direction built by differencing class means mixes two things. One part is shared by every entity that takes the value. The other part is specific to one entity. We separate them and steer with each part alone.

The shared part carries the causal effect in all three models. It raises the read out for both face regions by the same amount, +0.183 in gpt2, +0.190 in pythia, +0.317 in Qwen. The entity specific part moves the two regions apart in gpt2 by +0.016 with a dose response and p below 0.0001. In pythia and Qwen it moves them apart by 0.000. A random direction of matched norm does nothing in any model.

This explains an earlier observation. Injecting one region's direction changes another region's read out by the same amount as its own direction. The shared part dominates the vector, so the entity specific part has little room to act. That is a property of the direction we built. It is weak evidence about the model.

A linear probe does recover which region an attribute belongs to. The binding margin runs +0.04 to +0.15 shortly after a value is set and holds in all three models. The margin survives Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 180 tests, with 33 of 36 region pairs significant at distance zero on the four region dataset. Binding also decays. Past five sentences the within region signal drops below majority and the cross region read rises above it.

Model identity governs which variables clear a lexical baseline. gpt2 and Qwen both favour the eye variables and both fail on the lip variables. pythia reverses this. We report per model and treat the agreement between gpt2 and Qwen as two observations rather than a general law.

1

Introduction

Probing studies show that a variable is linearly recoverable from activations. They rarely show that the model's computation depends on it. The gap matters most for relations. Recovering that red appears somewhere in a scene is weaker than recovering that red is on the lips while brown is on the eyes. A domain with one object cannot separate these, because a property has only one thing to attach to.

We built a domain where several entities compete for the same values. A makeup routine is described one sentence at a time. Four face regions each carry a colour, a finish and a coverage level. Actions overwrite, blot, build up, sheer out or remove. The palette is shared across regions, so the colour word alone cannot say which region is red. Every sentence comes from an explicit state machine, so the label at each position is exact.

Three design choices make the task hard. Each value has ten realisations and most avoid the label word, so matte appears as "a chalk-flat surface" or "a dead-flat look that caught no light". Phrase banks and sentence skeletons are partitioned across splits, so a test narrative uses wording the probe has never seen. Eighteen per cent of sentences name a value without applying it, which misleads a bag of words model.

2

Related work

Li et al. (ICLR 2023) recovered Othello board state from a sequence model's residual stream. Nanda et al. (BlackboxNLP 2023) showed the same representation is linear once the target is framed in player relative terms, reaching above 99 per cent. We take a warning from that pair of results. How a target is parameterised changes what a probe finds. Othello has one board, so a bag of move tokens cannot reconstruct it. A bag of words goes much further on the question of which colour was mentioned. That asymmetry works against us and appears in our baselines.

Feng, Russell and Steinhardt (ICLR 2025) decode lexical concepts, identify a binding subspace and compose propositions such as WorksAs(Greg, nurse). Their setting asserts properties and reads them off. Ours overwrites them in sequence, which lets us ask how long a binding survives.

Kim and Schuster (ACL 2023) evaluate final state inference on ProPara and Recipes and find the ability appears only in models pretrained on large amounts of code. Their evaluation is behavioural. Ours is representational and causal. Their result also sets our expectations for scale. At 124M to 600M parameters we sit below the regime where strong effects are likely.

Our contribution is the decomposition test in section 4.1, the binding decay curve, and a domain that is multi entity, order destructive and free of surface overlap between splits. Linear probing, activation patching, control tasks and MDL are standard and we use them as they stand.

3

Method

Domain. Four entities: lips, eyes, cheeks, skin. Colour is drawn from bare, nude, pink, red, brown. Finish is drawn from none, matte, dewy, satin. Coverage is drawn from bare, light, full. Nine action kinds drive the state machine and two of them change nothing. Narratives run 5 to 16 sentences. About a quarter of positions hold a value that was overwritten earlier, so the first mention gives the wrong answer there.

Models. Seven candidates were benchmarked on load time, throughput, memory and a vocabulary check before use. meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B and google/gemma-2-2b are gated and remain unevaluated. The vocabulary check scores each domain term on three minimal pairs, for example "a matte finish reflects almost no ___" with light against sound. gpt2 is the primary model. pythia-410m and Qwen3-0.6B-Base are replication checks chosen to differ in family, depth and training data.

Probing. Residual stream at the last token of each sentence, every layer, logistic regression and MLP probes. Baselines are majority class, the model's own embedding layer, a mean pooled non contextual embedding and TF-IDF over the prefix text. Both read out conventions come from the same forward pass.

Intervention. We add a direction to the residual stream at a chosen layer and read the model's log odds over the state words. Control arms are a random direction at matched norm and the other region's direction at matched norm. Section 4.1 adds the decomposition described there.

Conventions. All p values are two sided. Intervals over narrative positions use a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole narratives, since positions inside a narrative share wording and state. Causal claims use alpha equal to 2. At alpha equal to 8 a random direction of matched norm also reaches significance, which puts the edit off distribution. MDL is reported over a sweep of probe regularisation, since a single value of C yields compression from 0.75 to 1.44 on the same data.

4

Results

Results run in order of how well they are supported. The first was designed to settle an ambiguity in our own earlier work.

4.1

The causal effect rides on the shared component

Shared effect in 3 of 3. Entity specific effect in 1 of 3.

A direction built as mean(dewy) − mean(matte) for the lips contains two parts. One part is what lips and eyes have in common when either turns dewy. The other part separates them. Write

shared s = (d_lips + d_eyes) / 2
differential r = (d_lips − d_eyes) / 2
so that d_lips = s + r

Both parts were rescaled to the norm of the full direction and injected on their own. If the model binds values to regions and uses that binding, r raises the lip read out and lowers the eye read out, giving a positive difference. If the model does not use it, r moves neither.

The shared part behaved the same way in all three models. It raised both read outs by nearly identical amounts, so the difference between regions stayed at zero. The entity specific part produced a difference of +0.016 in gpt2 with p below 0.0001 and a dose response that doubles at alpha 4. In pythia and Qwen the difference was 0.000. A random direction produced nothing anywhere.

The gpt2 effect is real and small. The shared part moves each read out by +0.183. The entity specific part moves the regions apart by +0.016, about a tenth as much, while making up 28 per cent of the full direction by norm. That ratio is why injecting the wrong region's direction works about as well as the right one.

4.2

Binding is recoverable by a probe and decays with distance

Replicates in 3 of 3

A probe trained on one region and read against another sits at that region's majority rate. The within region probe sits well above majority. The gap between them is the binding margin. It is positive in all three models shortly after a value is set and peaks two sentences later.

The margin then falls. On the four region dataset it turns negative past five sentences, where the within region signal drops below majority and the cross region read rises above it. At that range the representation still carries the fact that a value occurred and has lost the region it applied to. Section 4.1 is our reason for treating this as correlational.

4.3

Two of three models agree on which variables beat a lexical baseline

gpt2 and Qwen agree. pythia reverses.

gpt2 and Qwen both clear the static embedding baseline on the two eye variables and both fall below it on the two lip variables. The sign matches on all four targets. pythia gives the opposite pattern, with its only significant gap on lips.color.

We reported an earlier version of this result from two models and concluded that nothing replicated. That conclusion came from picking pythia as the second model. A third model shows pythia is the outlier. The direction of a probing result still depends on the checkpoint, and a two model comparison can mislead in either direction.

4.6

Findings from gpt2 alone

Single model

The signal is linear. Accuracy holds flat across a 64 fold range of probe capacity from linear to MLP-512 on every target tested. Selectivity stays flat and MDL stays flat.

Entity count collapses the contextual colour code. A control dataset with two entities at the four entity narrative length keeps colour selectivity at +0.119 with the best layer at 2. Four entities at the same length give +0.016 with the best layer at 0. Length alone has no effect.

Binding decay inverts past distance 5. The four entity dataset needed for this was extracted for gpt2 only.

5

Discussion

Section 4.1 changes how we read every steering result in this study. A difference of means direction is built to separate two label groups. The component that separates them best is the one the groups differ on, which is the value. Everything entity specific sits in the residual. Steering with the full direction therefore tests the value component and says little about the binding component.

Three consequences follow. Wrong region controls are weak on their own, since a shared component guarantees they pass. A cosine between two class directions is a statement about the construction and not about the model. And a steering result that moves a read out is evidence for a value code, which is a weaker claim than binding.

The decomposition gives a sharper reading. In gpt2 the entity specific component is causally effective at about a tenth the strength of the value component. In pythia and Qwen it is not effective at the doses we can use without going off distribution. A larger model may change this, and the test costs one extra arm.

On replication, our earlier two model comparison concluded that no target replicated. The third model shows that conclusion was an artifact of which second model we picked. gpt2 and Qwen agree on the sign of all four targets. Adding a model changed the answer, which is the argument for running more than two.

6

Corrections

Fourteen claims were revised during this work. Five were our own overclaims and three were errors in our statistics or tooling. Several headline results have their current form because a first version looked too clean and was checked again.

The intervention shows the model does not bind

Withdrawn. The wrong region arm cannot separate two readings. Either the model has no usable binding, or our direction captured the shared value component and left the binding component out. Section 4.1 was built to separate them and shows the second reading is closer to right.

No target replicates across models

Withdrawn. This held for gpt2 against pythia. Qwen agrees with gpt2 on the sign of all four targets. pythia is the outlier.

Binding degrades with entity count

Withdrawn, then partly reinstated. The first version rested on an aggregate dominated by positions where the attribute was not decodable, so both probes sat at majority. Stratifying by distance fixed it. A length controlled dataset then supported the claim for the contextual code.

Colour is causally load bearing

Registered as a prediction and falsified in gpt2 at every distance and arm. It holds in pythia.

The shuffled order control is weak

A misreading of an average. Eight of twelve targets peak at the embedding layer and have no temporal structure to destroy. Conditioned on depth the control works, at −0.036 for contextual targets against −0.006 for embedding layer targets.

A biased test statistic

We used the target effect less the absolute value of the other region effect. The expectation of an absolute value of noise is positive, so the statistic went negative at small effect sizes as an artifact. Replaced with the signed paired difference.

MDL below the uniform code

First explained as non convergence, which was wrong. The cause is regularisation. Prequential coding punishes confident errors on the small early blocks. Swept over C, every target compresses at 1.24 to 1.44. MDL also cannot adjudicate our central claim, because it runs on a pool where wordings are shared.

A reversing intervention

lips.coverage moved the wrong way. The null value bare occurs at mean narrative position 3.65 against 7.5 to 7.7 for the other values, so its difference of means direction encodes position in the text. Our read out spec excluded the analogous null value for finish and included it for coverage. Fixed and the old number withdrawn.

Checks that changed nothing

Probe regularisation swept, with validation selecting C = 0.01 and test accuracy moving from 0.348 to 0.350. Benjamini-Hochberg across 180 tests leaves the headline count at 33 of 36. Training set halved, after which eyes.finish survives at p = 0.0005 and eyes.color does not at p = 0.178, so we report the latter as fragile.

7

Limitations

Three small models. 124M, 410M and 600M parameters. Kim and Schuster find entity tracking in procedural text needs code pretrained models at GPT-3.5 scale. Three models establish that the pattern varies and say little about the range over which it varies.

Synthetic text. The paraphrase disjoint split makes this a real generalisation test and the distractor sentences make it adversarial to bag of words. The text still comes from templates.

A weak behavioural read out. Without intervention the models report the true state at roughly chance, 0.32 for lips.finish in gpt2. Causal claims rest on paired changes in log odds and on a probe read out downstream of the edit. The honest description is that we manipulate an attribute representation. We have not shown how a model performs binding.

A bounded dose. The decomposition uses alpha 2 and 4. At alpha 8 a random direction also reaches significance. A larger entity specific effect could exist above that range and would be unreadable.

Single model rows. Section 4.6 was run on gpt2 alone. Given 4.3 those results should be treated as claims about one checkpoint.

8

Conclusion

Steering with a difference of means direction moves the value of an attribute. The component shared across entities carries that effect in all three models. The entity specific component moves the two regions apart in gpt2 at about a tenth the strength and does nothing in pythia or Qwen.

A probe recovers the binding, the margin decays with narrative distance, and the causal evidence for the model using that binding is thin. The defensible statement is that the entity specific component recovered by a difference of means is not required for the read out we tested.

Anyone reading a binding claim off a steering result should decompose the direction first. The shared component will pass a wrong region control on its own.
