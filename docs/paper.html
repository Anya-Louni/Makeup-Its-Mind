Abstract

Adding one face region's concept direction to a language model's residual stream moves its read-out of a different region just as much, and sometimes more. We observed this in four independent tests spanning two models and two attributes: gpt2 finish (+0.149 for the wrong region against +0.148 for the correct one), pythia finish (+0.195 against +0.147), and pythia colour (+0.069 against +0.032, where the wrong direction is 2.2 times stronger). A random direction of matched norm had no effect in any of them. The causal machinery is real, and it is indifferent to which entity the value belongs to.

The short version of our conclusion is that values are used, bindings are only present, and which specific findings hold depends on the model rather than the task.

We tested this in a synthetic makeup-routine domain designed to separate binding from state tracking. Four face regions each carry a colour, a finish and a coverage level, updated by a state machine whose actions overwrite earlier values. Some sentences mention a value without applying it, and the train and test splits share no surface forms at all. A linear probe does recover which region an attribute belongs to. The binding margin is +0.07 to +0.12 shortly after a value is set, it holds in both models, and it survives Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 180 tests, leaving 33 of 36 region pairs significant at distance zero. That evidence is correlational, and the intervention above indicates the model does not use it. Binding also decays. Past roughly five sentences the within-region signal falls below majority while the cross-region read rises above it, so the representation still carries the fact that a value occurred somewhere after it has lost where.

Finally, the pattern of which state variables beat a lexical baseline does not replicate across models. No target is significant in the same direction in both, and our most robust gpt2 result (eyes.finish, two-sided p < 0.0001) gives p = 0.64 in pythia. We report per model rather than claiming a property of the domain, and we regard this as the clearest methodological result of the study.

1

Introduction

A language model that predicts the next word well might maintain a model of the situation the words describe, or it might exploit surface regularities that correlate with that situation. Probing studies routinely establish that some variable is linearly recoverable from the activations. They establish far less often that the model's own computation depends on it.

The gap matters most for relations. Recovering that red is present somewhere in a scene is a weaker result than recovering that red is on the lips while brown is on the eyes. Single-object domains such as a board position or a scalar quantity cannot separate the two, since there is only one thing for a property to attach to.

We therefore built a domain in which several entities compete for the same attribute values. A makeup routine is described one sentence at a time. Four face regions each carry a colour, a finish and a coverage level, and actions overwrite, blot, build up, sheer out or remove. The palette is shared across regions by design, so the question of which region is red cannot be answered from the colour word alone. Every sentence is generated from an explicit state machine, so the ground truth at each position is exact.

Three design choices make the test difficult. Each attribute value has ten realisations, most of which avoid the literal label word, so matte appears as "a chalk-flat surface" or "a dead-flat look that caught no light". Phrase banks and sentence skeletons are both partitioned across splits, so a test narrative contains no wording the probe has seen. And 18 per cent of sentences mention a value without applying it, as in "she held a deep crimson up to the light and shook her head", which misleads a bag-of-words model.

2

Related work

Li et al. (ICLR 2023) recovered Othello board state from a sequence model's residual stream, and we follow their template. Nanda et al. (BlackboxNLP 2023) then showed the representation is linear once the target is reframed in player-relative terms, reaching above 99 per cent accuracy. We carry that as a warning: whether a world model looks linear can depend on how the target is parameterised. Othello has one board, though. A bag of move tokens cannot reconstruct a board, whereas a bag of words goes a long way on the question of which colour was mentioned. That asymmetry works against us and shows up directly in our baselines.

Feng, Russell and Steinhardt (ICLR 2025) decode lexical concepts, identify a binding subspace, and compose propositions such as WorksAs(Greg, nurse), generalising from templates to short stories and to Spanish. Their setting is largely static, in that properties are asserted and then read off. Ours is sequential and destructive, which lets us ask a question their setup does not pose: how long does a binding survive?

Kim and Schuster (ACL 2023) evaluate final-state inference on ProPara and Recipes, and find that only models pretrained on large amounts of code exhibit the ability. Their evaluation is behavioural where ours is representational and causal. Their result also sets expectations for scale. At 124M and 410M parameters we sit well below the regime where strong effects should be expected.

What is new here is binding decay over narrative distance, together with the finding that presence outlives binding, measured two independent ways. The domain is also new: multi-entity, order-destructive, with exact labels and no surface overlap between splits. The cross-model comparison is new in this setting as well. Linear probing of emergent state, binding as a probing question, activation patching, control tasks and MDL are all standard, and we use them as they stand.

3

Method

Domain. Four entities (lips, eyes, cheeks, skin), each with a colour drawn from bare, nude, pink, red and brown, a finish drawn from none, matte, dewy and satin, and a coverage level drawn from bare, light and full. Nine action kinds drive the state machine, two of which change nothing. Narratives run 5 to 16 sentences, and the label at position i is the state after executing actions 0 through i. About a quarter of positions are ones where the value was overwritten earlier, so the first mention gives the wrong answer there.

Model selection. Seven candidates were benchmarked on load time, throughput, memory and a vocabulary check before any were used. meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B and google/gemma-2-2b are gated on the Hub and are recorded as unevaluated. The vocabulary check scores each domain term on three minimal pairs, for example "a matte finish reflects almost no ___" with light against sound, because free-form definitions from a base model are unreliable evidence in either direction. We selected gpt2 as the primary model. pythia-410m serves as the replication check, chosen because it differs in family, depth and training data rather than because it is the next size up.

Probing. We take the residual stream at the last token of each sentence, at every layer, and fit logistic-regression and MLP probes against four baselines: majority class, the model's own embedding layer, a mean-pooled non-contextual embedding, and TF-IDF over the raw prefix text. Both read-out conventions, last token and mean-pooled, are stored from the same forward pass.

Intervention. We add a concept direction to the residual stream at a chosen layer and read out the model's own log-odds over the state words. Three control arms run alongside, and the third of them decided the paper. The first is the other region's read-out taken from the same forward pass. The second is a random direction at matched norm. The third is the other region's concept direction at matched norm.

Conventions. All p-values are two-sided. Intervals over narrative positions use a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole narratives, since positions within a narrative share wordings and state. Causal claims use α = 2, because at α = 8 even random directions of matched norm reach significance, which indicates the edit is off-distribution. MDL is reported over a sweep of probe regularisation, since a single arbitrary C yields compression anywhere from 0.75× to 1.44× on identical data.

4

Results

Results are ordered by how well supported they are, not by the order in which we ran them. The first result replicated in every test. The last were run on one model and are labelled accordingly.

4.1

The causal effect is not specific to an entity

Fails in both models, 4 of 4 tests

Injecting the other region's concept direction, at the same attribute, norm and layer, moves the read-out as much as the correct region's direction in all four tests. For pythia's colour it is 2.2 times stronger. A random direction at matched norm has no effect anywhere, so the effect depends on the direction. It simply does not depend on which entity the direction came from.

The geometry predicts this, and we should have checked it earlier. Difference-of-means directions for the same value on two different regions have cosine between 0.67 and 0.95. They are nearly the same vector, so they interchange.

4.2

There is a real causal effect on the attribute value

Replicates in both models

This is what gives 4.1 its force. The concept direction shifts the model's own log-odds of the steered value by +0.055 (95 per cent CI +0.041 to +0.070) in gpt2 and +0.147 (+0.123 to +0.171) in pythia, while a matched-norm random direction gives +0.014 and −0.009 respectively, neither significant. The mechanism exists and depends on the direction. It encodes what the value is, and carries no information about whose it is.

4.3

Binding is recoverable by a probe, and decays with distance

Replicates in both models, correlational evidence

A probe trained on one region and read against another sits at that region's majority rate, so it carries no information about the other region, while the within-region probe sits well above majority. That is correct binding, with four regions competing over a shared palette, and it holds in both models at +0.04 to +0.12 shortly after a value is set.

It then decays. Past roughly five sentences the within-region signal drops below majority while the cross-region read rises above it. The representation still knows the value occurred somewhere and has lost which region it applied to. Presence outlives binding. This evidence is correlational, and 4.1 is our reason for not claiming the model uses it.

4.4

Which attribute is causally load-bearing depends on the model

Diverges between models

We registered a prediction in the script header before the run: colour should show a causal effect at short distance and none at long distance, which would have explained an earlier null as dilution. In gpt2 the prediction failed, with no effect at either distance for any arm. In pythia the same experiment gives a clean significant effect of +0.032 (+0.017 to +0.045) against a random arm of −0.010, which is not significant.

Colour is inert in one model and load-bearing in the other. This is the second instance of the same lesson as Figure 1.

4.5

The baseline comparison does not replicate

Diverges between models

Two of four targets clear the static-embedding baseline in gpt2 and one of four in pythia, with no target significant in the same direction in both. Our most robust gpt2 result is not significant in pythia. We report this as a methodological finding: it is direct evidence that single-model probing results of this kind should be treated as claims about a checkpoint.

4.6

Three findings established on gpt2 alone

Single model, not generalised

The signal is linear. Accuracy is flat across a 64-fold range of probe capacity, from linear to MLP-512, on every target tested. Selectivity does not rise and MDL does not improve. Whatever is encoded is linearly available.

Entity count rather than narrative length collapses the contextual colour code. A control dataset with two entities held at the four-entity narrative length keeps colour selectivity at +0.119, with the best layer at 2. Four entities at the same length reduce it to +0.016, with the best layer at 0, which is the embedding layer. Length on its own has no effect.

Binding decay inverts past distance 5, as shown in Figure 4. The four-entity dataset this needs was not extracted for pythia.

5

Discussion

Why do two models trained on similar objectives diverge this sharply? The clearest structural difference is where each keeps its state code. gpt2 spreads across the embedding layer and the middle of the network, and on the four-entity dataset eight of twelve targets peak at layer 0, meaning the transformer contributes nothing over a bag of embeddings for those variables. pythia sits almost entirely at the output end, in layers 20 to 24 of 24.

The agreement figure sharpens the point. Over a held-out narrative the two models agree with each other on 37.5 per cent of region-attribute readings, which is less often than gpt2 agrees with the ground truth at 56.2 per cent. They behave like two different representations rather than two noisy measurements of one.

The implication for probing work is narrow and concrete. Every experiment here was run identically on both models, on the same data with the same splits, and the direction of the headline effect changed. A paper reporting which variables are decodable, at which layers, above which baselines, is reporting a property of a checkpoint until it shows otherwise. Showing otherwise cost us one extra model.

The finding that survived contact with a second model is the negative one. Section 4.1 replicated across two models and two attributes, four times out of four. Results of that shape travel further than positive ones, because they do not depend on an effect being large enough to detect twice.

6

Corrections and robustness

Thirteen claims were revised during this work. Four were our own overclaims, caught by controls we added after the fact, and three were errors in our own statistics or tooling. We list them because the sequence is part of the evidence. Several of the headline results exist in their current form because a first version looked too clean and was checked again.

The intervention demonstrates binding

It does not. The design varied which region was queried and never varied which region's direction was injected. Adding that arm reversed the interpretation and produced the result now in section 4.1.

Binding degrades with entity count

Withdrawn. The aggregate was dominated by positions where the attribute was not decodable at all, so both probes sat at majority and agreed for uninformative reasons. Stratifying by distance recovered a stronger and correct version. A length-controlled dataset later reinstated the entity-count claim for the contextual code specifically.

Colour is causally load-bearing

Registered as a prediction and then falsified in gpt2, at every distance and every arm. It was later found to hold in pythia, which is section 4.4.

The shuffled-order control is weak

A misreading of an average over a mixed population. Eight of twelve targets peak at the embedding layer and have no temporal structure to destroy. Conditioned on depth the control works, at −0.036 for contextual targets against −0.006 for embedding-layer targets.

A biased test statistic

We used the target effect minus the absolute value of the other-region effect. Since the expectation of an absolute value of noise is positive, this drove the statistic negative at small effect sizes as an artifact. We replaced it with the signed paired difference.

MDL below the uniform code

First explained as non-convergence, which was wrong. The cause is regularisation: prequential coding punishes confident errors on the small early blocks. Swept over C, every target compresses at 1.24× to 1.44×. MDL also cannot adjudicate our central claim, because it runs on a pool where wordings are shared.

A reversing intervention

lips.coverage moved the wrong way. The cause was not a sign error. The null value bare occurs at mean narrative position 3.65 against 7.5 to 7.7 for the other values, so its difference-of-means direction encodes position in the text. Our read-out specification excluded the analogous null value for finish and included it for coverage. Fixed, and the earlier number is withdrawn.

Unfair pooling explains the weak lip results

Tested by storing both read-out conventions from the same forward pass. Pooling raises the numbers and moves the best layer to 0, which is the embedding baseline itself. The negative result was real.

Checks that did not change a conclusion

Probe regularisation swept, with validation selecting C = 0.01 and test accuracy moving from 0.348 to 0.350. Multiple comparisons corrected with Benjamini-Hochberg across 180 tests, leaving the headline count unchanged at 33 of 36. Training set halved, after which eyes.finish survives at p = 0.0005 and eyes.color does not at p = 0.178, so we report the latter as fragile. Both p-value conventions computed and labelled throughout.

7

Limitations

Two small models. 124M and 410M parameters. Kim and Schuster find that entity tracking in procedural text needs code-pretrained models of GPT-3.5 scale, so this study sits well below the regime where strong effects should be expected. Two models is a minimum replication. It establishes that the pattern varies, and says nothing about the range over which it varies.

Synthetic text. The paraphrase-disjoint split makes this a real generalisation test and the distractor sentences make it adversarial to bag-of-words, but the text is generated from templates.

A weak behavioural read-out. Without intervention the models report the true state at roughly chance, 0.32 for lips.finish in gpt2. Causal claims therefore rest on paired changes in log-odds and on a probe read-out downstream of the edit, rather than on the model's own accuracy.

Single-model rows. Linearity, distance decay and the entity-count control were run on gpt2 alone. Given section 4.5 they should not be assumed to transfer, which is the same point we make about other single-model results and it applies to ours.

8

Conclusion

In two small language models an attribute's value is linearly decodable, causally load-bearing, and shared across entities. The binding of that value to a particular entity is linearly decodable, decays with narrative distance, and shows no sign of being used. Which specific variables clear a lexical baseline, at which layers, and which attributes are causally effective are properties of the model rather than of the task.

The most portable result is the negative one. Across four independent tests spanning two models and two attributes, steering with the wrong region's direction moved the model's answer as much as steering with the right one, while a matched-norm random direction did nothing. Anyone reading a binding claim off a probe should run that arm before believing it, ourselves included, since we did not until the result had already been written down the other way.
