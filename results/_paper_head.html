Abstract

A concept direction built by differencing class means mixes two things. One part is shared by every entity that takes the value. The other is specific to one entity. We separate them and steer with each part alone. The shared part carries the causal effect in all four models we tested, and it grows with size, from +0.183 at 124M parameters to +0.556 at 1.7B. The region specific part moves the two regions apart in gpt2 by +0.016, and by 0.000 in pythia-410m and Qwen3-0.6B. At 1.7B, three times the largest of those, it is still +0.007 with p = 0.36. The value code strengthens with scale and the binding code does not appear.

Before reading anything into that null we checked the measuring instrument. With no steering at all, we asked whether the behavioural read out can tell the two regions apart. Changing the lips value moves the lips read out. Changing the eyes value moves it by about the same amount. Across the three smaller models the mean ratio of sensitivity to leakage is 1.07, and at 1.7B one of the two read outs reaches 2.00. The read out answers a question about one region using the other region's value.

That result gates the rest. A steering experiment read through this instrument cannot separate a model that fails to bind from an instrument that fails to bind. Our earlier conclusion rested on that instrument, and we withdraw it.

What survives is the probe. A linear probe trained per region does recover which region an attribute belongs to, with a binding margin of +0.04 to +0.15 shortly after a value is set in all four models, surviving Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 180 tests. Four regions give twelve ordered region pairs per attribute and three attributes, so 36 tests per distance bin and 180 across five bins, and 33 of 36 clear correction at distance zero. The margin decays with narrative distance. On the four region dataset, which we extracted for gpt2 only, it turns negative past five sentences.

The representation improves with scale even where the output does not. At 1.7B all four state variables clear the lexical baseline, against two of four at 124M, and the binding margin is the highest we measured. One of the two read outs also begins to bind, reaching a sensitivity to leakage ratio of 2.00 against roughly 1.07 in the smaller models.

Feng and Steinhardt (ICLR 2024) find binding ID vectors in every sufficiently large model of the Pythia and LLaMA families. Our four models run 124M to 1.7B parameters. A null across that range is consistent with sitting below their threshold. It is not evidence against the mechanism, and it puts a lower bound on where to look.

1

Introduction

Probing studies show that a variable is linearly recoverable from activations. They rarely show that the model's computation depends on it. The gap matters most for relations. Recovering that red appears somewhere in a scene is weaker than recovering that red is on the lips while brown is on the eyes. A domain with one object cannot separate these, because a property has only one thing to attach to.

We built a domain where several entities compete for the same values. A makeup routine is described one sentence at a time. Four face regions each carry a colour, a finish and a coverage level. Actions overwrite, blot, build up, sheer out or remove. The palette is shared across regions, so the colour word alone cannot say which region is red. Every sentence comes from an explicit state machine, so the label at each position is exact.

Three design choices make the task hard. Each value has ten realisations and most avoid the label word, so matte appears as "a chalk-flat surface" or "a dead-flat look that caught no light". Phrase banks and sentence skeletons are partitioned across splits, so a test narrative uses wording the probe has never seen. Eighteen per cent of sentences name a value without applying it, which misleads a bag of words model.

2

Related work

Li et al. (ICLR 2023) recovered Othello board state from a sequence model's residual stream. Nanda et al. (BlackboxNLP 2023) showed the same representation is linear once the target is framed in player relative terms, reaching above 99 per cent. We take a warning from that pair of results. How a target is parameterised changes what a probe finds. Othello has one board, so a bag of move tokens cannot reconstruct it. A bag of words goes much further on the question of which colour was mentioned. That asymmetry works against us and appears in our baselines.

Feng, Russell and Steinhardt (ICLR 2025) decode lexical concepts, identify a binding subspace and compose propositions such as WorksAs(Greg, nurse). Their setting asserts properties and reads them off. Ours overwrites them in sequence, which lets us ask how long a binding survives.

Feng and Steinhardt, How do Language Models Bind Entities in Context? (ICLR 2024), is the closest prior work. They show that models attach binding ID vectors to entities and to their attributes, establish it causally, and find the vectors occupy a continuous subspace where distance tracks discernability. They report the mechanism in every sufficiently large model of the Pythia and LLaMA families. The paper does not name a cutoff we could verify, and pythia-410m sits inside our own set without showing one.

Li, Nye and Andreas, Implicit Representations of Meaning in Neural Language Models (ACL 2021), is the earliest close relative. In BART and T5 they find contextual representations that behave like models of entities as a discourse unfolds, supporting a linear read out of each entity's current properties and manipulable with predictable effects on generation. They work in Alchemy and TextWorld.

Kim and Schuster (ACL 2023) probe whether a model can infer an entity's final state from an initial description and a series of operations. Their data is a synthetic boxes and objects setup, with operations that move, remove and add objects. Their evaluation is behavioural where ours is representational and causal. They find the ability appears only in models pretrained on large amounts of code, which sets our expectations for scale. Our four models span 124M to 1.7B.

Against that, our decomposition is not a new account of how binding is represented. Feng and Steinhardt already factor entity from attribute and identify the binding carrier. What we add is a result about the tool rather than the model. A difference of class means, which is the usual way a steering direction gets built, mixes the two components by construction, and we measure the mix at 28 to 38 per cent region specific by norm across four models. The consequence is practical. A wrong region control is the standard specificity check in steering work, and it passes on the shared component alone, so it cannot support a binding claim. Separating the components costs one extra arm and no subspace estimation.

The binding decay curve and the domain are the other two pieces. Linear probing, activation patching, control tasks and MDL are standard and we use them as they stand.

3

Method

Domain. Four entities: lips, eyes, cheeks, skin. Colour is drawn from bare, nude, pink, red, brown. Finish is drawn from none, matte, dewy, satin. Coverage is drawn from bare, light, full. Nine action kinds drive the state machine and two of them change nothing. Narratives run 5 to 16 sentences. About a quarter of positions hold a value that was overwritten earlier, so the first mention gives the wrong answer there.

Models. Seven candidates were benchmarked on load time, throughput, memory and a vocabulary check before use. meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B and google/gemma-2-2b are gated and remain unevaluated. The vocabulary check scores each domain term on three minimal pairs, for example "a matte finish reflects almost no ___" with light against sound. gpt2 is the primary model. pythia-410m and Qwen3-0.6B-Base are replication checks chosen to differ in family, depth and training data. Qwen3-1.7B-Base is the scale arm, roughly three times the largest of the others, run on a GPU against byte identical narratives.

Probing. Residual stream at the last token of each sentence, every layer, logistic regression and MLP probes. Baselines are majority class, the model's own embedding layer, a mean pooled non contextual embedding and TF-IDF over the prefix text. The TF-IDF baseline never exceeds the static embedding baseline on any target in any model, so the static embedding is the one we report against throughout. Both read out conventions come from the same forward pass.

Intervention. We add a direction to the residual stream at a chosen layer and read the model's log odds over the state words. Control arms are a random direction at matched norm and the other region's direction at matched norm. Section 4.2 adds the decomposition described there.

Conventions. All p values are two sided. Intervals over narrative positions use a cluster bootstrap that resamples whole narratives, since positions inside a narrative share wording and state. Causal claims use a steering gain of g = 2, where g is a multiple of the full direction's norm. We write it as g instead of alpha to keep it apart from the significance level. At g = 8 a random direction of matched norm also reaches significance, which puts the edit off distribution. MDL is reported over a sweep of probe regularisation, since a single value of C yields compression from 0.75 to 1.44 on the same data.

4

Results

Results run in order of how well they are supported. The first was designed to check whether the measurement could register the thing the rest of the study set out to test.

4.1

Binding is present in the residual stream and absent from the output

Holds in the three smaller models, weakens at 1.7B

A null from steering means nothing if the measurement cannot register a difference, so we checked without steering anything. Take held out positions where the two regions carry different finishes and compare the model's log odds across all four assignments of two values to two regions. Sensitivity changes the value of the region we asked about. Leakage changes the other region's value.

The three smaller models move about equally on both, with a mean sensitivity to leakage ratio of 1.07. Sensitivity is significant everywhere, so the instrument registers changes and the null is not a floor effect. The read out answers a question about one region using the other region's value.

The read out here is the model's own next token distribution, so this is a fact about behaviour and not about our tooling. Set against 4.3, where a probe recovers the binding at a margin of +0.04 to +0.15, the pair gives the cleaner statement. Binding is linearly decodable in the residual stream and the output does not use it. Feng and Steinhardt find binding ID vectors only above a scale threshold, and our models run 124M to 1.7B, so present but unused is what we should expect to find down here.

Averaging the ratio hides structure worth reporting. In each model one region drives both read outs at nearly the same strength, and which region varies. gpt2 and pythia answer both questions from the eyes value. Qwen answers both from the lips value.

We tested the obvious explanation and it holds in one model of three. In Qwen the value of whichever region was acted on more often predicts the read out better than either region does, which is a salience account. In gpt2 and pythia the eyes value dominates and neither recency nor frequency of action explains it. We report a fixed per model preference with no mechanism behind it. One limit: we tested recency of state changes, not recency of mentions, since the distractor sentences name values without applying them.

4.2

The causal effect rides on the shared component

Shared effect in 4 of 4. Region specific effect in 1 of 4.

A direction built as mean(dewy) − mean(matte) for the lips contains two parts. One part is what lips and eyes have in common when either turns dewy. The other separates them. Both parts were rescaled to the norm of the full direction and injected on their own.

If the model binds values to regions and uses that binding, r raises the lip read out and lowers the eye read out, giving a positive difference. If the model does not use it, r moves neither.

The shared part raised both read outs by nearly identical amounts in the three smaller models, so the difference between regions stayed at zero. The entity specific part produced a difference of +0.016 in gpt2 with p below 0.0001 and a dose response that doubles at g = 4. In pythia-410m and Qwen3-0.6B the difference was 0.000, and at 1.7B +0.007 with p = 0.36. A random direction produced nothing anywhere.

Qwen3-1.7B breaks the pattern on the shared arm. There the shared part alone moved the two read outs apart by +0.038 [+0.005, +0.070] with p = 0.0216, and the full direction moved them apart by +0.042. The entity specific part accounts for +0.007 of that and is not significant, so the whole asymmetry at 1.7B sits in the component that carries no region information. A direction built to be identical for lips and eyes acts more strongly on one of them. The size is a tenth of the shared arm's push on each read out, which is +0.556 and +0.518, so it reads as unequal sensitivity of the two read outs to the same edit and not as binding. It is also the one place where the full direction looks region selective, and the decomposition shows that appearance comes from the shared half.

The gpt2 effect is real and small. The shared part moves each read out by +0.183. The entity specific part moves the regions apart by +0.016, about a tenth as much, while making up 28 per cent of the full direction by norm. That ratio is why injecting the wrong region's direction works about as well as the right one.

The same caveat from 4.1 applies to this positive result, and we state it instead of keeping the one number that came out our way. The gpt2 effect was measured through the read out that 4.1 shows does not bind. A differential of +0.016 could come from lexical content carried in r and not from a binding the model uses. We report it as a small effect of unclear origin, and a read out that binds would be needed to attribute it.

At 1.7B one read out does bind, so we can put r through it. Section 4.1 gives the eyes read out a sensitivity to leakage ratio of 2.00 in that model. Here the target is the lips and the other region is the eyes, so r should push the eyes read out down. It pushes it up, by +0.062 [+0.037, +0.088] with p below 0.0001. The push on the lips read out is +0.069, so r raises both read outs by almost the same amount. Read through the one channel that tracks its own region, the entity specific component behaves as a weak value push with no region attached.

4.3

Binding is recoverable by a probe and decays with distance

Replicates in 4 of 4

A probe trained on one region and read against another sits at that region's majority rate. The within region probe sits well above majority. The gap between them is the binding margin. It is positive in all four models shortly after a value is set and peaks two sentences later.

The margin then falls. Figure 5 shows the two region dataset, run on all four models, where the margin stays positive throughout and is largest at 1.7B. The sign change past five sentences appears only on the four region dataset, which we extracted for gpt2 alone, and is reported in 4.7. Section 4.2 is our reason for treating this as correlational.

4.4

Which variables beat a lexical baseline depends on the model, and improves with size

gpt2 and Qwen agree. pythia reverses.

gpt2 and Qwen3-0.6B both clear the static embedding baseline on the two eye variables and both fall below it on the two lip variables. The sign matches on all four targets. pythia gives the opposite pattern, with its only significant gap on lips.color. Qwen3-1.7B clears the baseline on all four, which is the first model here to do so.

The evidence is thin. Two of the four targets are not significant in any model, sign agreement on four targets is a weak test, and our own robustness check lists eyes.color as fragile, since it falls to p = 0.178 when the training set is halved. We report the agreement and do not lean on it.

We reported an earlier version of this result from two models and concluded that nothing replicated. That conclusion came from picking pythia as the second model. A third model shows pythia is the outlier. The direction of a probing result still depends on the checkpoint, and a two model comparison can mislead in either direction.

4.5

The models place their state code in different layers

Differs across all four

gpt2 spreads across the embedding layer and the middle of the network. On the four region dataset eight of twelve targets peak at layer 0, where the transformer adds nothing over a bag of embeddings. pythia sits at layers 20 to 24 of 24. Qwen3-0.6B sits at layers 16 to 26 of 28 and Qwen3-1.7B at layers 18 to 28 of 28. The models also disagree with each other on a held out narrative more often than they disagree with the truth.

4.7

Findings from gpt2 alone

Single model

The signal is linear. Accuracy holds flat across a 64 fold range of probe capacity from linear to MLP-512 on every target tested. Selectivity stays flat and MDL stays flat.

Entity count collapses the contextual colour code. A control dataset with two entities at the four entity narrative length keeps colour selectivity at +0.119 with the best layer at 2. Four entities at the same length give +0.016 with the best layer at 0. Length alone has no effect.

Binding decay inverts past distance 5. The four entity dataset needed for this was extracted for gpt2 only.

4.8

What scale changes, and what it leaves alone

Four models, 124M to 1.7B

Qwen3-1.7B is roughly three times the largest of the other three and was run on the same narratives, byte for byte. Three things move with size and one does not.

The value code strengthens. The shared component's causal effect rises monotonically, +0.183, +0.190, +0.317, +0.556.

The representation improves. At 1.7B all four state variables clear the static embedding baseline, at +0.072, +0.098, +0.062 and +0.098, every one significant. The lip variables that fell below the baseline in gpt2 and Qwen3-0.6B now clear it. Binding margins are the highest of any model, peaking at +0.199.

The read out starts to bind. The eyes read out reaches a sensitivity to leakage ratio of 2.00, sensitivity +0.699 against leakage +0.350. The lips read out is still at 1.09, so this is one of two.

The binding code does not appear. The region specific component is +0.007 with p = 0.36. Across a fourteen fold range of parameters it is significant only in the smallest model, where 4.2 argues the effect could be lexical.

The salience account from 4.1 fits Qwen3-0.6B alone. At 1.7B the eyes value dominates again, +0.667, with recency at +0.606, so it is not a size trend.

5

Discussion

The result we would defend is that the binding is present and the model does not use it. A probe reads which region an attribute belongs to at a margin of +0.04 to +0.15 in all four models. The model's own next token distribution answers a question about one region using the other region's value, at a sensitivity to leakage ratio of 1.07 in the three smaller models. Steering the component that carries the region information moves nothing in three of four. Three measurements, two of them causal, point the same way.

Section 4.2 explains why the earlier reading was wrong. A difference of means direction is built to separate two label groups. The component that separates them best is the one the groups differ on, which is the value. Everything entity specific sits in the residual. Steering with the full direction tests the value component and says little about the binding component.

Three consequences follow. Wrong region controls are weak on their own, since a shared component guarantees they pass. A cosine between two class directions describes the construction and not the model. And a steering result that moves a read out is evidence for a value code, which is weaker than binding.

Scale moves the representation and leaves the use of it alone. From 124M to 1.7B the value code's causal effect triples, all four state variables come to clear the lexical baseline, the binding margin reaches its highest value, and one of the two read outs starts to bind. The region specific component stays inert across the whole range. Feng and Steinhardt find binding ID vectors above a scale threshold, and 1.7B may still sit below it, so the reading we prefer is that these models build the binding before they learn to read it.

On replication, our earlier two model comparison concluded that no target replicated. The third model shows that conclusion came from which second model we picked. gpt2 and Qwen agree on the sign of all four targets. Adding a model changed the answer, which is the argument for running more than two.

6

Corrections

Twenty one claims were revised during this work. The nine below are the ones that changed a reported number or a conclusion. The full log sits in docs/CORRECTIONS.md in the repository. Several headline results have their current form because a first version looked too clean and was checked again.

The intervention shows the model does not bind

Withdrawn. The wrong region arm cannot separate two readings. Either the model has no usable binding, or our direction captured the shared value component and left the binding component out. Section 4.2 was built to separate them and shows the second reading is closer to right.

No target replicates across models

Withdrawn. This held for gpt2 against pythia. Qwen agrees with gpt2 on the sign of all four targets. pythia is the outlier.

Binding degrades with entity count

Withdrawn, then partly reinstated. The first version rested on an aggregate dominated by positions where the attribute was not decodable, so both probes sat at majority. Stratifying by distance fixed it. A length controlled dataset then supported the claim for the contextual code.

Colour is causally load bearing

Registered as a prediction and falsified in gpt2 at every distance and arm. It holds in pythia.

The shuffled order control is weak

A misreading of an average. Eight of twelve targets peak at the embedding layer and have no temporal structure to destroy. Conditioned on depth the control works, at −0.036 for contextual targets against −0.006 for embedding layer targets.

A biased test statistic

We used the target effect less the absolute value of the other region effect. The expectation of an absolute value of noise is positive, so the statistic went negative at small effect sizes as an artifact. Replaced with the signed paired difference.

MDL below the uniform code

First explained as non convergence, which was wrong. The cause is regularisation. Prequential coding punishes confident errors on the small early blocks. Swept over C, every target compresses at 1.24 to 1.44. MDL also cannot adjudicate our central claim, because it runs on a pool where wordings are shared.

A reversing intervention

lips.coverage moved the wrong way. The null value bare occurs at mean narrative position 3.65 against 7.5 to 7.7 for the other values, so its difference of means direction encodes position in the text. Our read out spec excluded the analogous null value for finish and included it for coverage. Fixed and the old number withdrawn.

Checks that changed nothing

Probe regularisation swept, with validation selecting C = 0.01 and test accuracy moving from 0.348 to 0.350. Benjamini-Hochberg across 180 tests leaves the headline count at 33 of 36. Training set halved, after which eyes.finish survives at p = 0.0005 and eyes.color does not at p = 0.178, so we report the latter as fragile.

7

Limitations

Four small models. 124M, 410M, 600M and 1.7B parameters. Kim and Schuster find entity tracking in procedural text needs code pretrained models at GPT-3.5 scale. Four models establish that the pattern varies with size in some places and holds flat in others, across a fourteen fold parameter range.

Synthetic text. The paraphrase disjoint split makes this a real generalisation test and the distractor sentences make it adversarial to bag of words. The text still comes from templates.

A weak behavioural read out. Without intervention the models report the true state at roughly chance, 0.32 for lips.finish in gpt2. Causal claims rest on paired changes in log odds and on a probe read out downstream of the edit. The honest description is that we manipulate an attribute representation. We have not shown how a model performs binding.

A bounded dose. The decomposition uses g = 2 and g = 4. At g = 8 a random direction also reaches significance. A larger entity specific effect could exist above that range and would be unreadable.

Single model rows. Section 4.7 was run on gpt2 alone. Given 4.4 those results should be treated as claims about one checkpoint.

8

Conclusion

A probe reads which face region an attribute belongs to in all four models. The models' own output answers a question about one region using the other region's value. Steering the component that carries the region information moves the two read outs by the same amount in three of four models. The binding is in the residual stream and the read out does not use it.

Steering with a difference of means direction moves the value of an attribute. The component shared across entities carries that effect in all four models and triples in strength from 124M to 1.7B. The entity specific component moves the two regions apart in gpt2 at about a tenth the strength and does nothing in the other three.

Scale improves what is there and leaves its use flat. Across a fourteen fold parameter range the representation gets better on every measure we have, and the region specific component stays inert. Anyone reading a binding claim off a steering result should decompose the direction first. The shared component will pass a wrong region control on its own.

Code, data generators and result files are released with this write up. Twenty four analysis scripts, eight modules, four orchestration chains and a corrections log.
Conventions: two sided p values, cluster bootstrap over narratives, intervention at gain g = 2, MDL over a regularisation sweep.
